Can reduction alone hold a wheelie?

A working simulation study of a rider-supervised controller that may remove engine torque—but may never add more than the rider requests.

Target reference: 2020 BMW S1000RR K67 + riderModel status: reduced-order, not vehicle-validatedRevision: November 4, 2026

01 / QUESTION

The constraint changes everything.

An ordinary pitch controller can command more or less drive. This controller has only half of that authority: it can cap positive torque below the rider’s request. If the motorcycle falls below the reference and the rider is not asking for enough torque, the control input required by the mathematics simply does not exist.

T_permitted⁺(t) = min[T_rider⁺(t), T_ceiling(t)]
0 ≤ T_permitted⁺(t) ≤ T_rider⁺(t)

The rider initiates the wheelie. The supervisor observes pitch, pitch rate, wheel speed, engine speed and derived quantities. It reduces a torque ceiling as the state approaches the reference. Closing the grip always remains effective, including explicit negative engine braking. This is neither autonomous throttle control nor a proposal to modify a BMW ECU.

The simulator is allowed to fail. A low-friction run may never lift. A delayed actuator may overshoot the safety boundary. A rider who closes the throttle may make a positive pitch reference unreachable. Those are research outcomes, not bugs to hide.

The live application. The technical drawing, wheel contacts, force arrow, telemetry and plots all read the integrated plant state.

02 / PHYSICS

A hybrid rigid-body model.

The model has two modes joined by physical contact conditions: both wheels constrained to the road, and a rear-supported wheelie. It does not infer pitch from throttle or play an authored animation.

Forces used by the longitudinal and load-transfer model. Positive pitch is nose-up.

Geometry and lift-off

Let b be the longitudinal COM distance forward from the rear contact, h its height relative to the axle line, L wheelbase and rᵣ rear radius. At pitch θ:

x_COM = b cos θ − h sin θ
z_COM = rᵣ + b sin θ + h cos θ
clearance_front = max(0, L sin θ)

On the ground, longitudinal acceleration follows Newton’s second law. Quasi-static load transfer supplies the contact switching condition:

m a = Fₓ − ½ρCᴅA v|v| − Cᵣᵣmg sign(v)
N𝒇 = (mgb − mah) / L     Nᵣ = mg − N𝒇
lift-off when unconstrained N𝒇 ≤ 0

Wheelie dynamics and gravity

With front contact absent, the reduced pitch equation balances the acceleration-induced moment, gravity and lumped pitch damping about the rear support:

Iθ θ̈ = m a z_COM(θ) − m g x_COM(θ) − cθ θ̇

The acceleration required to hold a fixed angle is therefore a_hold = g x_COM / z_COM. Zero torque does not freeze pitch: angular velocity persists, engine torque decays over time, and gravity supplies a restoring moment while the COM remains forward of the support. Touchdown projects negative pitch to zero and applies a small angular restitution.

Tire, wheel and engine

κ = (rᵣωᵣ − v) / max(|v|, 2)
Fₓ = μNᵣ tanh(Cκ κ / μNᵣ)   ⇒   |Fₓ| ≤ μNᵣ
I𝓌 ω̇ᵣ = T𝓌 − Fₓrᵣ − T_loss
T𝓌 = T_engine · i_primary · i_gear · i_final · η

The smooth brush-like tire approximation captures slip and traction saturation without claiming a measured K67 tire fit. Grip is mapped nonlinearly to an estimated torque curve. A queue, first-order response and slew limit represent transport delay and engine dynamics. The published 197 kg road-ready mass, 1.441 m wheelbase and representative 113 N·m maximum torque are separated from estimated COM, inertia, aerodynamics and latency in configuration.

03 / CONTROL

Supervision, not replacement.

Every controller produces a ceiling. The plant enforces the rider constraint again, so an implementation error in a controller still cannot generate extra positive torque.

Baselines

No assist establishes the plant response. Threshold linearly removes torque above a pitch boundary. Both are valuable precisely because they expose how much performance comes from the controller rather than the scenario.

Equilibrium-feedforward PD/PID

The feedback controller maps the hold acceleration through the selected gear to a steady engine-torque estimate. A bounded reference-rate governor permits upward motion after lift, then commands the pitch rate toward zero as the target approaches. Feedback corrects pitch and rate error around that physical operating point. Anti-windup respects both zero torque and the rider ceiling.

T_eq(θ) ≈ m[a_hold(θ) + Cᵣᵣg] rᵣ / (i_total η)
q_ref = clamp[kq(θ_ref − θ), −q_max, q_max]
T_ceiling = clamp[T_eq − Kp(θ−θ_ref) − Kd(q−q_ref) − Ki∫e dt, 0, T_rider]

Delay-aware and predictive control

The delay-aware controller evaluates a forward pitch estimate. The predictive controller uses constrained direct shooting over candidate ceiling sequences, a nonlinear pitch prediction, actuator response, slew limits, and penalties on tracking error, rate, intervention and safety-boundary approach. Invalid state causes a fail-closed zero ceiling. It is deliberately dependency-free and auditable; it is not presented as equivalent to a production CasADi/acados/MATMPC implementation.

A negative result matters: the included predictive baseline did not beat the tuned PD controller. Its coarse action grid and approximate internal model are likely causes. Calling it “NMPC” does not make it superior.
04 / SOFTWARE

One plant, two ways to run.

The browser loop and batch experiments share the same physics and controller modules. That prevents the animation from becoming a separate, easier model.

Fixed-step semi-implicit Euler integration runs at 2 ms. Sensors/controllers update independently at 10 ms. Seeded pseudo-random noise makes experiments repeatable. The application has no runtime dependencies; a small Node HTTP server preserves ES module behavior. Exports are ordinary CSV and JSON.

Passive telemetry path

The CSV importer accepts explicit-unit names such as time_s, pitch_deg, engine_rpm and throttle_pct. It normalizes units, rejects non-increasing timestamps, tolerates missing optional channels and computes nearest-time RMSE against simulation. It sends no commands and assumes no BMW channel exists until a real logger proves it.

05 / EXPERIMENTS

Success is narrow.

The same plant was exercised with unassisted acceleration, ideal stabilization, insufficient and excessive rider demand, 100 ms delay, sensor noise, altered COM, low traction and a command fault. Every safety failure stops at the declared boundary instead of integrating meaningless rotations.

The ideal case demonstrates the mechanism: enough rider request establishes lift; the controller removes the surplus and converges around the nonzero torque needed to oppose gravity. The insufficient-demand case demonstrates the one-sided reachability boundary. Low friction prevents the acceleration required for lift. Delay and faults allow angular momentum to accumulate after the controller has decided to intervene.

Interpretation discipline: a final pitch near target is not sufficient. Classification also considers lift occurrence, time within tolerance, overshoot, angular rate, intervention, saturation, constraint violations and declared model boundaries.
06 / ANSWER

Yes—but only conditionally.

Within this reduced model, a reduction-only controller can maintain a nonzero pitch reference when the rider supplies a torque request above the instantaneous holding requirement and the controller can remove the surplus before delay and angular momentum carry the state beyond recovery.

It cannot create the acceleration needed to raise or recover the front when rider demand, traction, gear or engine operating point make that acceleration unavailable. It cannot instantly erase pitch rate. It is best understood as an overshoot manager and torque governor around a rider-generated maneuver, not an autonomous balancing input.

What physically permits stabilization

  • The rider request creates a positive torque interval containing the required holding torque.
  • Tire friction supports the longitudinal force without saturation.
  • Pitch rate remains small enough that actuator lag can remove torque before the safety boundary.
  • The geometry keeps the COM forward of the rear contact in the modeled operating range, so gravity supplies a restoring moment.
  • State estimation, road slope and parameter mismatch are accurate enough for the feedforward term.

What this project cannot establish

The model cannot determine safe real-bike gains, actual K67 ECU delay, true combined COM/inertia, tire behavior, road-slope robustness, suspension interaction, sensor-estimation quality or whether a usable external torque-control interface exists. The published anti-wheelie work shows that reduced internal models and delay compensation can be useful, but those results on other platforms do not validate these BMW parameters.

07 / NEXT

Turn estimates into measurements.

The next meaningful improvement is not a more elaborate optimizer. It is parameter identification and model validation from passive data.

  1. Static geometry: measure axle loads with rider positions to infer longitudinal COM; use safe fixtures or published laboratory methods for height.
  2. Rotational properties: estimate wheel inertia and combined pitch inertia with documented bench methods.
  3. Passive logging: collect timestamped RPM, wheel speeds, grip demand, acceleration and independently measured pitch where legally and safely possible. Do not assume a generic channel is present.
  4. Time alignment: estimate sensor and actuator delay separately; avoid folding every mismatch into one fitted constant.
  5. Hold-out validation: fit on one set of non-wheelie longitudinal maneuvers and report prediction error on another before studying lift.
  6. Model ladder: compare this reduced model with a suspension-aware multibody model; only then revisit a higher-quality NMPC implementation.

The import module already validates timestamps and units, synchronizes by nearest time, reports missing channels and computes measured-versus-simulated error. It remains deliberately offline.
